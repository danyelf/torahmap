Each week in synagogue, the Torah reading is followed by a passage from the Prophets — the “haftarah”.

<!-- stop: overview_nev_1 | camera: Neviim | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi -->

The Torahmap colors the Torah portion and its corresponding Haftarah in the same color.

<!-- stop: overview_nev_2 | camera: Neviim | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi -->

Which parts of the Prophets have become a part of the annual cycle?

<!-- stop: isaiah | camera: Isaiah | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi -->

The book of Isaiah has many Haftarah selections. 

<!-- stop: comfort | camera: Isaiah.50.1 | zoom: 2 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi -->

In the latter half of the book, the repeated blue colors show that the readings are near each other.

<!-- stop: comfort_2 | camera: Isaiah.50.1 | zoom: 2 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi -->

These are the Haftarahs of Consolation, read in the late summer alongside the book of Deuteronomy.

Tisha b’Av, a fast day mourning ancient disaster, has just passed.

<!-- stop: consolation_1 | camera: Isaiah.52.1 | zoom: 1 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi | verse: Isaiah.40.1 | reading: Vaetchanan -->

The readings switch to words of hope from Isaiah. *“Comfort, comfort My people.”*

<!-- stop: consolation_2 | camera: Isaiah.52.1 | zoom: 1 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi | reading: Eikev | verse: Isaiah.51.3 -->

*Truly GOD has comforted...*

<!-- stop: consolation_3 | camera: Isaiah.52.1 | zoom: 1 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi | reading: Re'eh | verse: Isaiah.54.11 -->

*Unhappy, storm-tossed one, uncomforted!*

<!-- stop: consolation_4 | camera: Isaiah.52.1 | zoom: 1 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi | reading: Shoftim | verse: Isaiah.51.12 -->

*I am the One who comforts you!*

<!-- stop: consolation_5 | camera: Isaiah.52.1 | zoom: 1 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi | reading: Ki Teitzei | verse: Isaiah.54.1 -->

*Shout aloud for joy...*

<!-- stop: consolation_6 | camera: Isaiah.52.1 | zoom: 1 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi | reading: Ki Tavo | verse: Isaiah.60.1 -->

*Arise, shine, for your light has dawned!*

<!-- stop: consolation_7 | camera: Isaiah.52.1 | zoom: 1 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi | reading: Nitzavim | verse: Isaiah.61.10 -->

*I greatly rejoice in GOD...*

<!-- stop: song_haazinu | camera: II.Samuel.22.1 | zoom: 2 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi | reading: Ha'Azinu -->

II Samuel 22, David’s song of deliverance, is read twice. Once beside the song of Moses,
and once on the seventh day of Passover, beside the Song at the Sea.

<!-- stop: coverage | camera: Neviim | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi -->

Overall, a year of haftarot reads about one verse in five from the Prophets.

<!-- stop: minor_prophets | camera: Jonah.1.1 | zoom: 1 | overlay: haftarah | custom: ashkenazi -->

While Obadiah and Jonah are read in full, Nahum, Zephaniah, and Haggai are never read at all.
